NEOGOV Round 5 — Katharina Matsui (CRO) Prep

What this interview is

Tuesday 2026-07-07 afternoon. Your fifth-round conversation for the NEOGOV Revenue AI Architect role. Interviewer: Katharina Matsui, Chief Revenue Officer at NEOGOV.

This is the ultimate stakeholder gate. Nick vetted revenue-ops fluency in Round 2. Zach vetted application-stack fluency in Round 3. Phil vetted business-partner fit in Round 4. The critical context — NEOGOV strategy she was hired to execute

Vocabulary you'll need

Before the strategic context, a quick vocabulary primer for the acronyms Katharina lives in:

  • EQT — EQT Group, a Stockholm-based global private equity firm; one of the largest PE firms in the world
  • CPP — CPP Investments (Canada Pension Plan Investment Board), one of the world's largest institutional investors; manages the retirement assets of ~21 million Canadians. Sophisticated, long-term, growth-oriented
  • ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue. The north-star SaaS metric — the annualized value of subscription revenue. This is the number Katharina's board measures her against. "AI-driven ARR expansion" means "using AI to grow the annualized recurring revenue"
  • OTE — On-Target Earnings. Base salary + expected commission at 100% quota. Standard sales-comp vocabulary. (For your own comp discussions later: base is the wife-blessed floor, per your own principles.)
  • "Land and expand" — the SaaS motion of getting a foothold customer (small initial deal, one product) then growing that account over time (more seats, more products, more use cases). This is the public-safety vertical roll-up story below.
  • "Sponsor-to-sponsor deal" — a private-equity acquisition where one PE consortium sells to another. NEOGOV was sold from Warburg Pincus + Carlyle to EQT + CPP.

"Advancing product innovation and AI capabilities" + North America scale-up

Translation into practical terms: EQT + CPP paid a premium price because they believe NEOGOV can grow ARR (annual recurring revenue) faster than it has been — specifically through AI product expansion and by capturing more of the North American public-sector market.

Katharina was hired ~11 months later — presumably as part of the leadership team EQT is assembling to execute this thesis. The Revenue AI Architect role she's now interviewing candidates for is a direct expression of that thesis — she needs to be able to tell her board and CEO Shane Evangelist that she hired an AI Architect and it drove measurable ARR growth.

Question 3 — "Walk me through a production AI system you've built end-to-end"

Apply the E2E pipeline template. Do NOT narrow to a specific piece. Walk the full 10 steps. This is the specific failure mode that cost you at PG&E. Don't repeat it here.

You have TWO strong options. Pick based on what she's probing for:

Option A — the current Unify enterprise agentic AI platform work (Vigil-shape). More recent, more directly relevant to her Falkon interest, exact vocabulary match to her assistive-AI posture. Stay at architectural level — do NOT disclose client business specifics. Suggested walkthrough:

  1. Problem — build an enterprise-grade agentic AI platform for a revenue-intelligence use case; the platform has to be trustworthy enough for enterprise use, integrate with existing systems of record, and be operable by the client's team after handoff
  2. Data pipeline — modular adapter-based integration layer that puts each external system of record (Salesforce CRM live, others as needed) behind a single connector contract; enterprise data flows in and out through the adapter surface
  3. Data prep — normalized through the adapter contract; each connector handles the source-specific translation so the agent layer sees consistent shapes
  4. Architecture — agentic AI platform with LLM agent-evaluation frameworks as first-class citizens (not bolted on); human-in-the-loop safety invariants as design constraints, not after-thought guardrails
  5. Adaptation — prompt engineering with structured output; agent-evaluation harness runs continuously
  6. Evaluation — the agent-evaluation framework IS the CI; safety-invariant checks fail the build if agent output would violate the invariants
  7. Deployment — controlled-access, cloud-native, with federated identity so enterprise IAM policies extend into the platform without duplication
  8. Monitoring — grounded-ness of agent output tracked continuously; drift monitoring across model providers and prompt versions
  9. Feedback loops — human-in-the-loop reviewers correct agent output; corrections feed back into the evaluation harness and prompt tuning
  10. Refresh + methodology — I've also pioneered a multi-agent development methodology spanning research, drafting, and adversarial review that shapes how the whole team builds against this platform. Refresh is continuous.

Option B — Echo Outlook Add-Ins (safer to detail, past employer, complete). Use if she wants specific business-outcome numbers or if the conversation drifts toward "show me sales-facing AI adoption":

  1. Problem — sellers at a freight brokerage were spending 5+ min per email quoting rates and hunting shipments; deal velocity was the constraint on revenue
  2. Data pipeline — real-time email intake via Outlook, historical rate data from data warehouse, shipment inventory from the marketplace
  3. Data prep — normalize customer / lane / shipment features; RAG index over historical quotes and current inventory
  4. Architecture — Claude Instant 1.0 with structured-output prompting; RAG over the fixed reference material; user-side Add-Ins for both sales-rep and carrier flows
  5. Adaptation — prompt engineering with few-shot examples; as-needed prompt version updates
  6. Evaluation — labeled set of gold-standard responses; measured against sales-rep-reviewed corrections
  7. Deployment — beta production rollout within Outlook, so it lived where sellers already worked
  8. Monitoring — drift observation (Claude Instant 1.0's behavior shifted as Anthropic refined training); user feedback rates; time-to-response distribution
  9. Feedback loops — sales-rep correction mechanism I built into the flow — corrections flowed back into golden set and drove prompt updates
  10. Refresh — supervised junior data scientists in evaluating newer Claude models as they became available

Which to pick: Option A (Vigil-shape) is stronger for the CRO/platform-architect framing. Option B (Echo) is safer if she asks about "sales-facing" specifically or wants a completed shipped system.

Question 4 — "How would you approach the first 90 days if you joined?"

Answer shape:

"Not by proposing architecture. By spending real time with your sellers — sitting in on calls if I can, watching how they use Salesforce today, understanding what they actually do all day. And by talking with your RevOps team about where forecast slips and where signal is missing. And by understanding the public-safety cross-sell picture — because that's where the highest-leverage AI signals probably live for your sponsors. I do bring a specific pattern from what I'm building right now — enterprise agentic AI platform with Salesforce-behind-adapter integration, LLM agent-evaluation frameworks, human-in-the-loop safety invariants. That's an operational blueprint I could adapt for NEOGOV. But the priority in the first 90 days is context, not blueprint. Once I have that context, then I'd talk architecture."

The public-safety cross-sell reference signals homework. The Vigil-shape platform reference signals "I bring proven architecture, not theory." The "sellers-first" posture signals partnership.

Question 5 — "What's your view on assistive AI vs. agentic AI for RevOps?"

She has publicly staked out the "assistive not agentic" position. Agree with the sensible parts and go deeper — don't push agentic hype:

"Assistive is the right posture for revenue systems specifically. Agentic AI works when the loss function is clear and consequences of a bad action are recoverable. Revenue conversations don't have that shape — a rep loses trust with a customer once, that customer is gone. Assistive AI amplifies the seller's judgment; agentic AI substitutes for it. For NEOGOV specifically — public-sector buyers who are risk-averse — assistive is the only defensible posture. That doesn't mean unsophisticated: what I'm building right now at Unify is an agentic AI platform with human-in-the-loop safety invariants as first-class design constraints — the invariants keep agent output grounded and trustworthy. Assistive done right requires the same rigor as anything agentic: evaluation harnesses, drift monitoring, versioned releases, and observability."

That signals: you agree with her, you understand the reasoning, you're not intellectually captured by AI hype, AND you're currently building the exact operational shape she'd want for NEOGOV — human-in-the-loop safety invariants at the platform layer.

The Vigil framing here is especially powerful because it says "the current work I'm doing is architecturally aligned with your posture."

Question 6 — "How do you measure whether the AI is working?"

Answer shape:

"Two layers. Engineering layer: precision, recall, drift over time, cost per inference, latency. Those matter for engineering but not for you. The layer that matters for your seat is ARR-adjacent: revenue velocity change on AI-touched pipeline vs. matched pipeline that isn't AI-touched, forecast accuracy delta over time, seller-hours saved on repeatable work, cross-sell attach rate movement in the public-safety stack. Instrument both from day one. Report to you in your language, not mine."

Why "ARR-adjacent" lands: it signals you know ARR is her board-level scoreboard, and that every AI capability ultimately needs to trace back to it. That's the CRO altitude answer.

What NOT to do

  • Don't lead with the multi-physics PhD or 40-year framing. Wrong audience for a CRO conversation.
  • Don't lead with methodology. No PGA, no Delta E, no VLLMs, no MCP. She's wary of AI hype vocabulary.
  • Don't push agentic AI. She's publicly staked out the assistive position. Match her posture.
  • Don't over-explain foundation models. She uses AI at the strategy layer, not the implementation layer. Speak in business outcomes.
  • Don't disparage the current stack or previous choices. She inherits Matt Redden's org and Culture Amp exit was hard — she'll be sensitive to team-culture signals.
  • Don't tell her how to run a revenue engine. She has been CRO at Culture Amp, SVP at OneTrust, VP at Twilio. Ask, don't teach.
  • Don't reference the PG&E outcome. No context in which that lands well.
  • Don't mention Integrating Consulting / 1099. Offer-stage only.
  • Don't telegraph the Unify timeline. Still private.
  • Don't over-detail Vigil / RIE. You can mention high-level that you're on a revenue-intelligence AI project inside Unify — that's directly relevant to her Falkon advisorship interest — but don't share client-confidential details.
  • Don't skip fundamentals when asked "how would you build X." Per the E2E pipeline template. This is the specific miss that cost you at PG&E. Don't repeat it.
  • Don't ramble. CROs are time-conscious. Answer crisply, land the point, then stop.
