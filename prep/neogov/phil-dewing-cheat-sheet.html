10-Minute Phil Dewing Cram Cheat Sheet

Read at 12:45 PM. Walk in at 1:00 PM.

1. Who Phil is

  • VP of Sales at NEOGOV. Rounds 2 (Nick) and 3 (Zach) said yes. Phil is the business-partner test.
  • His sellers are the end users of everything the Revenue AI Architect would build.
  • He owns adoption risk. He can veto tools that add work to a seller's day.
  • He owns the business-outcome case internally. When the CEO asks "what did we get," Phil answers.

What Phil is measuring: would I want this person embedded with my sales leaders?

2. The lens

  • Not testing your architecture — Nick and Zach already did that.
  • Testing your seller-empathy, business-outcome fluency, and adoption instincts.
  • Testing whether you'll be a partner to Sales or an ivory-tower architect.

3. The single strongest credential — Echo Outlook Add-Ins

Echo Global Logistics = freight brokerage = brutally sales-driven company. You shipped AI to actual sellers there at production scale that they actually used.

Canonical phrasing to have ready (~30 seconds):

"I built two full-stack LLM-based AI Outlook Add-Ins at Echo Global Logistics — the freight brokerage. One on the sales-rep side for rapid rate-quote replies to customers. One on the carrier side that helped actual carriers find shipments they could ask to take at an offered price. Combined, 30,000+ daily requests. Per-request handling went from 5+ minutes to under 15 seconds. Both sides of the market got faster."

Why it lands: both sides of the market + time compression at scale + real production adoption + freight brokerage context. That's exactly the shape Phil wants.

4. What Phil cares about (in order)

  1. Will your AI help my sellers hit their number? (quota, deal velocity, forecast accuracy)
  2. Will it be intrusive or additive to seller workflow? (Every seller has a tool graveyard.)
  3. How fast will results show up? (Sales VPs think in quarters, not years.)
  4. Will you respect his sellers' time in architecture choices? (Not "log 15 fields before AI helps.")
  5. Can you speak business, not architecture? (Board briefings. Customer meetings. Non-technical rooms.)

5. The five most likely questions — answer shapes

Q1. "Tell me about your sales-facing AI experience." → Deploy the Echo Outlook Add-Ins canonical phrasing above. ~30 sec. Land and stop.

Q2. "First 90 days if you joined?" → "Not by proposing architecture. By spending time with your sellers, watching how they use Salesforce, understanding what they do all day — and by talking with you about where your forecast slips and where signal is missing. Architecture comes after that context."

Q3. "What if my sellers push back on adoption?" → "Then I want to understand why. Seller pushback on AI comes from three places: adds work instead of removing it, wrong often enough to be untrustworthy, or competes with the actual selling conversation. All three are architecture problems, not seller problems. Rather learn that in week two than quarter two."

Q4. "How do you measure whether the AI is working?" → "Two layers. Engineering layer: precision, recall, cost, latency. Business layer that matters for your seat: deal velocity change, forecast accuracy delta, seller-hours saved, win-rate movement on AI-touched deals vs. matched deals it didn't touch. Instrument both from day one."

Q5. "How do you think about AI vs. the seller?" (the fear-of-replacement question in disguise) → "AI works best when it hands sellers superpowers, not replacements. At Echo, sellers spent 5+ minutes drafting rate quotes — nobody enjoys that work. AI took it, gave them 15 seconds instead, and they used the reclaimed time to talk to customers. That's the shape."

6. Questions to ask Phil (deploy 2-3)

★ Opening after his intro: "What does a typical NEOGOV seller's day look like right now? Where's the friction that AI could take off their plate?"

Middle-conversation options:

  • "How's forecasting currently working, and where would AI signal help vs. hurt the accuracy of your commit?"
  • "What's your expectation on adoption cadence? Sellers only use tools that make their day easier — how do you think about the transition period?"

★ The closer (last 2-3 min): "What does 'AI succeeded' look like from your seat — beyond the CRM dashboards? What would you tell your board 6 months in?"

7. What to avoid

  • Don't lead with the multi-physics PhD. Wrong audience.
  • Don't lead with architecture, methodology, or foundation-model fluency. Nick and Zach's lane. Speak outcomes.
  • Don't tell Phil how sales should be done. He knows his motion. Ask, don't teach.
  • Don't mention 1099 / Integrating Consulting. Offer-stage only.
  • Don't telegraph Unify timeline. Private.
  • Don't get technical to prove you're smart. He knows you are — that's why he's talking to you.
  • Don't be defensive about AI hype. Agree with the sensible skepticism, anchor on production work you've shipped.

8. Closing posture

Warm. Curious. Business-minded. Listen more than you talk.

Ground everything in seller time and business outcomes. Treat Phil as a peer collaborator on a shared question — how do we make NEOGOV's sellers more effective — not as an evaluator you're trying to convince.

Three things to demonstrate in 30 minutes:

  1. You understand sellers as people, not workflow endpoints (Echo store + adoption question posture)
  2. You speak in business outcomes, not architecture (velocity, forecast, win-rate, adoption)
  3. You'd be a partner to Sales, not a competitor for its attention (asking about his motion, not proposing solutions)

If Phil says yes, the technical loop is closed. This is the gate.

You've got this.
